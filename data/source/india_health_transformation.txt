India's Health Transformation
Published by Press Information Bureau, PIB Delhi, on 06 June 2026. This local text is a cleaned copy of the assignment source page and is used only to seed the demo index.

Overview and Universal Health Coverage
In the past 12 years, the Government has strengthened India's health system. It has insured over 44 crore families and operationalised over 1.86 lakh primary care centres. Generic medicines are available at rates 50-90 percent below market price through more than 18,000 Jan Aushadhi Kendras. Over 47 crore telemedicine consultations have been delivered. Medical colleges have more than doubled and 12 new AIIMS are functional since 2014. Traditional medicine has been integrated into the public health system. Maternal and child mortality rates have dropped significantly since 2014, TB incidence has fallen, and malaria mortality is down 78 percent. The document frames these gains as progress toward universal health coverage. A National Statistical Office 2025 survey of over 1.39 lakh households found that more Indians are seeking medical care, many outpatient visits at government facilities involve no payment, and about half of hospitalised patients pay below Rs. 1,100. The article attributes the shift to increased government spending, public health upgrades, insurance schemes, digital health infrastructure, and expanded medical education.

Ayushman Bharat and AB-PMJAY
Ayushman Bharat, launched in 2018, is described as the central universal health coverage programme. It is built around preventive, promotive, curative, and digital healthcare. The first pillar is Ayushman Bharat - Pradhan Mantri Jan Aarogya Yojana, or AB-PMJAY. The National Health Policy 2017 recognised new health challenges such as non-communicable diseases and rising treatment costs, and AB-PMJAY was launched in 2018 to address catastrophic medical spending. The scheme provides free public health insurance up to Rs. 5 lakh per family per year for socio-economically deprived families, covering about 40 percent of the population or around 12 crore families. In October 2024, Ayushman Bharat Vay Vandana extended insurance coverage to all senior citizens above 70 years. The insurance covers consultations, hospitalisations, and specialised treatment including cancer and heart diseases through empanelled public and private hospitals. The document reports about 40,000 claims processed daily for more than 1,900 treatment packages. It lists 44.14 crore Ayushman Cards, 12.03 crore covered hospitalisations, treatment worth Rs. 1,80,435 crore, 36,218 empanelled hospitals, and 1.20 crore enrolled senior citizens under Vay Vandana as of June 5, 2026. The Ayushman App supports eligibility checks, e-card downloads, wallet balance monitoring, hospital search, and grievances.

Ayushman Arogya Mandirs
The second pillar is primary care through Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, or AAMs. These centres scale primary healthcare for minor ailments, outreach programmes, and community care. AAMs provide comprehensive preventive, promotive, curative, rehabilitative, and palliative care. Each AAM offers 12 free services beyond maternal and child healthcare, including screening and management of diseases, oral care, eye care, ear-nose-throat care, mental health support, teleconsultations, first-level emergency and trauma care, essential medicines, and diagnostics. The centres have recorded cumulative footfall of more than 540 crore. The document lists more than 1.86 lakh functional AAMs, including 1.34 lakh Sub Health Centres, 24,483 Primary Health Centres, 5,474 Urban Primary Health Centres, 12,259 AYUSH centres, and 9,758 Urban Health and Wellness Centres as of June 5, 2026. AAMs also support Community Health Workers and ASHAs, who spread awareness, check on patients, and track community health. The article gives Lalmati sub-centre in Assam as an example: after upgrade to an AAM, the centre added non-communicable disease screening, mental health support, oral and eye care, emergency services, and trained Community Health Officers, leading to zero preventable maternal and child mortality since 2024.

PM-ABHIM and Pandemic Preparedness
The third pillar is Pradhan Mantri Ayushman Bharat Health Infrastructure Mission, or PM-ABHIM. It was launched on October 25, 2021 as a pan-national programme for a resilient, accessible, and self-reliant public health system. Its total financial outlay is Rs. 64,180 crore for financial years 2021-22 to 2025-26. The mission builds capacity across the health system by supporting 23,224 rural health and wellness centres in 10 high-focus states, establishing 13,736 urban health and wellness centres, creating 3,389 block public health units in 11 high-focus states, setting up 744 integrated public health labs in all districts, and establishing 631 critical care hospital blocks in districts with population above 5 lakh. Beyond buildings, PM-ABHIM invests in bio-security preparedness, pandemic research, disease surveillance, outbreak response and preparedness at 50 points of entry into the country, and critical care blocks with 150 beds across 12 central institutions. The document connects this pillar to the role of primary health centres during public health emergencies such as COVID-19, where they support disease surveillance, screening, and treatment.

Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission
The fourth pillar is Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, or ABDM, launched in September 2021. It is presented as a citizen-centric digital infrastructure that gives citizens ownership of their health data and makes it portable, accessible, and usable across providers. ABDM creates a common source of truth for health facilities, professionals, and pharmacies, helping continuity of care and clinical decisions. At its centre is the Ayushman Bharat Health Account, or ABHA, a unique 14-digit health identity number linked to a citizen's health records. With consent, these records can be accessed by healthcare professionals across the ABDM network for paperless care. The document says ABDM supports evidence-based decision-making at population level. The ABHA app, run by the National Health Authority, provides QR-based appointment registration to reduce queues and verify patient data. The page reports 20.49 crore registrations on the app and 27,328 facilities connected across 36 states and union territories as of March 31, 2026.

National Health Mission and Maternal Child Health
The National Health Mission, with National Rural Health Mission and National Urban Health Mission, delivers targeted programmes for diseases, populations, and health challenges. The document says NHM has improved outcomes in maternal and child health, disease elimination, and immunisation. Community health workers from primary healthcare centres deliver services with better tools and data. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan, launched in 2016, provides free quality antenatal check-ups at government facilities. More than 7.47 crore pregnant women were examined and 22,349 facilities provided PMSMA services as of June 5, 2026. Janani Suraksha Yojana supports facility-based childbirth for poor pregnant women, particularly below-poverty-line, scheduled caste, and scheduled tribe communities. Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram entitles pregnant women at public facilities to free delivery, drugs, diagnostics, diet, and transport with no out-of-pocket cost, and it was extended in 2014 to antenatal and post-natal complications. Other NHM programmes include Surakshit Matritva Aashwasan, the Midwifery Initiative, Stillbirth Surveillance and Response, Home Based Newborn Care, Home Based Care of Young Child, Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram, Anaemia Mukt Bharat, weekly iron and folic acid supplementation, Mothers' Absolute Affection, Adolescent Friendly Health Centres, and the Menstrual Hygiene Scheme.

Mission Indradhanush and U-WIN
The document says vaccination of newborns and pregnant mothers is central to health and longevity. The Universal Immunisation Programme targets 2.67 crore newborns and 2.9 crore pregnant women each year, offering free vaccines against 12 diseases. Mission Indradhanush was launched in 2014 as a catch-up programme for children and pregnant women who were not vaccinated or only partly vaccinated. Through Mission Indradhanush, 5.46 crore children and 1.32 crore pregnant women who were not immunised before were vaccinated. Across routine immunisation, more than 95 percent of children aged 12-23 months received vaccinations at a public health facility in 2023-24. The share of zero-dose children fell from 0.11 percent of the population in 2023 to 0.06 percent in 2024. India's immunisation programmes also contributed to elimination of maternal and neonatal tetanus, certified by the World Health Organisation in May 2015. U-WIN, launched for pan-India rollout in 2024, digitally records every vaccination event, tracks due doses, and provides permanent portable health records. As of March 2026, 11.87 crore children and 3.96 crore pregnant women were registered. A 2026 cervical cancer prevention campaign provided free HPV vaccine to girls aged 14 years.

Communicable Disease Programmes
The document says NHM programmes have reduced communicable diseases over the past 12 years, with measurable outcomes for tuberculosis, malaria, leprosy, and other diseases. Under the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme, TB incidence and deaths declined faster than the global rate of decline. A community support network of more than 3.78 lakh Nik-shay Mitras supported more than 20 lakh patients with nutritional, vocational, and diagnostic support, including more than 45 lakh food baskets as of December 2025. Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan, launched in September 2022, is the community-led component of the TB programme and turned TB elimination into a people's movement. Since December 7, 2024, more than 20 crore people were screened for TB and more than 28 lakh patients were diagnosed. For malaria, the 2016 National Framework for Malaria Elimination and the National Strategic Plan for Malaria Elimination 2023-2027 introduced enhanced surveillance, a test-treat-track approach, and real-time data tracking. Other results include a 74.5 percent decline in HIV-AIDS mother-to-child transmission between 2010 and 2024, Kala-Azar reduction in endemic blocks, Japanese Encephalitis case fatality reduction from 17.6 percent in 2014 to 7.1 percent in 2024, dengue case fatality reduction to 0.13 percent in 2024, progress in lymphatic filariasis districts clearing transmission assessment, and leprosy elimination progress.

COVID-19 Response
India's COVID-19 response is described as proactive. The government started airport screening before the first domestic case was reported, formed a vaccine task force in April 2020, and was among the first globally to introduce Rapid Antigen Tests. On January 16, 2021, India launched the National COVID-19 Vaccination Programme, one of the world's largest vaccination drives. More than 220 crore doses, including two indigenous vaccines, were administered free of charge at government schools, clinics, hospitals, and other venues. Certificates were digitised and delivered through the COWIN platform. Infrastructure was scaled rapidly: testing labs grew from 14 to 3,400, ICU beds increased from 2,168 to 1.45 lakh, oxygen-supported beds rose from 50,583 to 5.15 lakh, and domestic PPE kit manufacturing scaled from zero to more than 5 lakh kits per day. Over 1,563 PSA oxygen plants were sanctioned, and around 900 Oxygen Express trains transported more than 36,840 tonnes of liquid medical oxygen. Under Vaccine Maitri, India supplied vaccines to nearly 100 countries, including free vaccines to 48 countries, delivering about 300 million doses.

Non-Communicable Disease Screening and Treatment
The document says non-communicable diseases such as heart disease, diabetes, cancer, and stroke account for 60 percent of all deaths in India. To address this, the National Programme for Prevention and Control of NCDs strengthens infrastructure for early detection, diagnosis, and treatment. Ayushman Arogya Mandirs conduct community screenings for NCDs including cancer. More than 60 crore screenings were conducted for oral, breast, and cervical cancers at AAMs. The source reports 35.3 crore oral cancer screenings, with 2.3 lakh detected and about 2 lakh being treated; more than 16.5 crore breast cancer screenings; 8.73 crore cervical cancer screenings, with 1.1 lakh women diagnosed and about 97,000 being treated; 41.5 crore hypertension screenings since 2017, with 7.1 crore diagnosed and 5.7 crore notified; and 41.3 crore diabetes screenings, with 4.7 crore found diabetic and 3.4 crore being treated. Cancer care has expanded through 19 State Cancer Institutes, 20 Tertiary Cancer Care Centres, cancer facilities approved in all 22 new AIIMS, and over 600 cancer registry sites plus over 100 stroke registry sites. The Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme, rolled out in 2016, provides free dialysis to poor patients. It served 31.74 lakh patients, conducted more than 4 crore haemodialysis sessions across 1,816 centres, and saved an estimated Rs. 10,102.25 crore in out-of-pocket spending as of June 5, 2026.

NCD Prevention and Tobacco Control
The article links NCD prevention to lifestyle, food safety, activity, and tobacco control. Eat Right India, launched in July 2018, promotes safe, healthy, and sustainable food through hygiene at food establishments, healthy eating, and food free from contaminants. The initiative certified 182 Clean Street Food Hubs, 546 Fruits and Vegetables Markets, and 411 railway stations as of June 5, 2026, and collected more than 21 thousand kilos of used cooking oil in 2024-25. Fit India, launched in 2019, encourages physical activity and daily fitness. Fit India Sundays on Cycle promotes cycling in urban spaces; the campaign engaged more than 30 lakh citizens across over 2.8 lakh locations. Tobacco control is treated as a major public health measure because tobacco use claims more than 13 lakh lives annually and is a leading cancer cause. The National Tobacco Control Programme achieved a 17.3 percent reduction in overall tobacco use over the past decade, while tobacco use among school-going children declined from 14.6 percent in 2009 to 8.5 percent in 2019. More than 2,000 Tobacco Cessation Centres were established. The National Tobacco QuitLine assisted over 6.5 lakh people from June 2016 to April 2026, and sustained counselling helped 34.5 percent of callers quit tobacco. Tobacco Free Youth Campaigns helped more than 3.09 lakh educational institutions and 39,000 villages achieve tobacco-free status.

Affordable Medicines Diagnostics and Emergency Transport
The document says healthcare becomes accessible when patients can afford medicines, diagnostics, and transport. The Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana provides quality-assured generic medicines through Jan Aushadhi Kendras, usually 50-80 percent cheaper than branded prices. AMRIT Pharmacies, launched in 2015, provide life-saving and essential medicines at 50-90 percent discounts. They have benefited over 6.85 crore patients through more than 255 outlets, saving around Rs. 8,400 crore. The Free Essential Diagnostics Initiative was launched in 2015 under NHM to reduce out-of-pocket diagnostic spending. It prescribes 9 tests at Sub-Centre level, 19 at Primary Healthcare Centres, 39 at Community Health Centres, and 57 at District Hospital level, covering haematology, serology, biochemistry, clinical pathology, microbiology, radiology, and cardiology. Andhra Pradesh was the first state to implement the initiative in 2016 through NTR Vaidya Pariksha across 13 districts. It extended free laboratory and radiology services to 1,182 primary healthcare centres, 192 community health centres, 31 Area Hospitals, and 8 District Hospitals. Tele-radiology was introduced at 120 facilities, and daily volumes included 45,000 to 55,000 tests and 1,200 X-rays. Emergency transport through Dial 108 and Dial 102 operates across 35 states and union territories, supported by 3,044 Advanced Life Support ambulances, 15,283 Basic Life Support ambulances, 3,918 Patient Transport Vehicles, 19 boats, and 81 bikes.

Digital and Last-Mile Health Services
Since 2014, the government has expanded last-mile delivery through digital health technology. eSanjeevani, India's national telemedicine platform, lets citizens in underserved or remote areas consult doctors through smartphones, computers, or nearby primary and sub-health centres. Launched in November 2019, it was initially for doctor-to-doctor consultation but demand increased when the pandemic limited hospital visits. It now connects people directly to specialists in top medical institutes. Since launch, more than 47 crore calls were recorded and more than 2.34 lakh healthcare providers, including doctors, specialists, super-specialists, government hospitals, medical colleges, and Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, were onboarded. Tele-MANAS, launched in October 2022, provides telephone-based counselling, psychotherapy, psychiatric consultation, referral, and urgent care services for mental health. It is available in 20 languages across all 36 states and union territories. The Tele-MANAS mobile app launched in October 2024 and improved accessibility for visually impaired people through a user-friendly interface and a toll-free phone line that does not require screen use. The network includes 53 Tele-Manas Cells and 23 Mentoring Institutes. ICMR's i-DRONE service, launched in 2021, uses drones to transport medicines, vaccines, and blood samples in remote or difficult terrain, delivering 22,000 medicines over 7,700 kilometres through 65 healthcare centres.

Artificial Intelligence Medical Education and AYUSH
The government is deploying artificial intelligence in healthcare to improve equity, accessibility, affordability, and speed. AI-powered Clinical Decision Support Systems assist doctors during eSanjeevani consultations through structured data capture and clinical alerts. The document reports 28.2 crore eSanjeevani consultations benefited from standardised AI-enabled support from April 2023 to November 2025. The Cough Against TB tool uses cough analysis to identify likely tuberculosis cases and reported an additional 12-16 percent TB cases that could have been missed through conventional screening. More than 1.62 lakh people were screened with the tool from March 2023 to November 2025. MadhuNetrAI supports automated diabetic retinopathy screening, preventing avoidable blindness without needing a specialist ophthalmologist at screening. It was implemented across 38 facilities in 11 states and screened more than 14,000 retinal images, benefiting 7,100 patients as of December 2025. The article also says medical education has expanded to meet demand for health professionals. Nursing expansion includes 157 new nursing colleges alongside new medical colleges, adding about 15,700 nursing graduates annually. Since 2014, B.Sc. Nursing seats rose 53 percent to 1,27,290 and M.Sc. Nursing seats rose 39 percent to 14,986 as of June 2025. AYUSH was formally integrated into public health after the Ministry of AYUSH was formed in November 2014. As of 2025, 942 AYUSH institutions were opened, and AYUSH care was mainstreamed across 13,093 NHM co-located facilities as of December 2025. The AYUSH Grid will digitally connect AYUSH hospitals and laboratories, and an AYUSH Visa introduced in July 2023 supports foreign nationals seeking treatment in India.

Viksit Bharat 2047
The document concludes that investments in medical infrastructure, primary care, digital health, disease programmes, and medical education have strengthened India's health sector and moved the country toward universal health coverage. It connects a healthier population to economic progress: fewer people are pushed into poverty by medical bills, workers are not lost to preventable disease, and children who survive early years and grow up healthier can learn, earn, and contribute. The benefits are expected to compound across generations. Children born into a system with near-universal immunisation, accessible primary care, early disease screening, affordable treatment, and portable digital records may inherit a stronger health baseline. The article positions these health changes as part of the vision of Viksit Bharat at 2047: a healthier, stronger, and more prosperous nation for every citizen. It also links the health system's progress to the public service motto Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas. In that frame, universal health coverage is not treated as one scheme alone. It is shown as the combined result of insurance protection, stronger primary care, pandemic preparedness, digital records, targeted disease programmes, affordable medicines, emergency transport, telemedicine, artificial intelligence support, medical education, and integrated traditional medicine across public facilities and communities nationwide.
